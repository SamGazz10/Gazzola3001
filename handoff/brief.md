# MMA3001 Numerical Methods and Machine Learning - Individual Project Brief (25% of unit mark)

Assessed primarily on: demonstrated individual learning; quality and organisation of computational work; validity of evaluation; justification for technical decisions; lessons drawn from results; ability to communicate clearly and professionally. An unsuccessful or partially successful method can still demonstrate excellent engineering work if validated honestly, limitations identified, lessons explained and improvements proposed.

Each project must include: a clearly defined engineering problem relevant to mechanical or aerospace engineering; at least one numerical method and/or ML technique taught in the unit; evidence of model development, validation and verification, sensitivity analysis or optimisation, interpretation of results in an engineering context; reproducible workflow using version control (GitHub required); appropriate use of computational tools (Python, Jupyter, numerical libraries, ML frameworks); acknowledgement and details of AI usage.

## Project Requirements - GitHub with code, documentation and readme. The Documentation acts as the project report and should include:

1. Engineering problem: engineering context; problem addressed; why it matters; intended use of the computational solution; limitations of the selected scope.
2. Inputs and outputs: input data accepted; expected format, units and domain of inputs; outputs produced; meaning and intended use of each output; how invalid, missing or unsupported inputs are handled. Top-level I/O in documentation; later function-level I/O (docstrings and documentation generators).
3. Computational solution: functioning and reproducible workflow. Explain how the method works at an appropriate level; why it was selected; important assumptions; settings or parameters; how it was implemented; known limitations.
4. Alternative solutions: identify and compare credible alternatives; at least one alternative or baseline; comparison may address accuracy, robustness, computational time, memory use, implementation complexity, interpretability, scalability, suitability. Demonstrate final approach selected using evidence rather than preference.
5. Validation: clear and justified validation method (known ground truth; withheld test data; independent experimental data; repeated measurements; trusted benchmark; established computational method; cross-validation; physical constraints/conservation; sensitivity and robustness testing; manual inspection with defined criteria). Must explain: what was validated; why the method is appropriate; which metrics or acceptance criteria were used; whether validation information influenced model selection; what the validation cannot establish. A numerical result or visually convincing output is not sufficient evidence of accuracy.
6. Optimisation and performance: analyse and where appropriate improve performance (accuracy, runtime, memory, robustness, numerical stability, settings, input resolution, data requirements, repeatability, scalability, trade-offs). Use profilers, timing routines, and where appropriate estimates of arithmetic intensity and FLOPs to explain and justify performance. Report must compare the original/baseline solution with the improved/selected solution and justify the final design.
7. AI use and reflection: disclose all material AI use. AI reflection must explain: which AI tools; what for; approximate level of AI contribution; why AI used for those tasks; how AI-generated material was checked; which important decisions remained the student's; errors, limitations or unhelpful suggestions produced by AI; how AI affected understanding or workflow. No AI allowed during presentation and interview.

## Assessment Deliverables
Written Project Documentation: individual written document of 5-10 pages maximum (references and appendices do not count). Must communicate: 1 engineering problem; 2 inputs, outputs and data; 3 computational approach; 4 validation method and results; 5 comparison and optimisation work; 6 limitations and lessons learned; 7 AI-use reflection; 8 references. Forms the basis of the presentation; mark based on presentation and ability to answer questions.

GitHub repository must include: 1 ReadMe; 2 HTML documentation of source code (pdoc or similar); 3 licensing agreement; 4 source code - functional main branch, code includes inline comments and docstrings; 5 test functions and test report (pytest). Zipped clone submitted via Moodle.

Presentation: individual 5 minute presentation + 3 minutes questions. Must include: brief explanation of the engineering problem - primary input and output; demonstration of GitHub and structure, documentation of code; evidence of code validation and testing; chosen computational approach and why; principal limitations or lessons learned; student's use and evaluation of AI.
Example questions: What physical or engineering system did your numerical model represent, and what assumptions were required to make it tractable? Which parameters or boundary conditions had the greatest influence on the behaviour of your solution, and how did you verify they were realistic? How did you ensure numerical stability? Describe any convergence criteria used and why appropriate. Main sources of numerical error and how quantified or controlled? How did you verify your numerical method was implemented correctly (analytical solutions, manufactured solutions, benchmark problems)? Which part of your solution is most vulnerable to numerical failure, and how would you mitigate that risk?

## Rubric (Excellent level)
- Engineering problem, inputs and outputs: clear, professional, complete description; context well explained; problem precisely defined; importance and intended use explicit; scope and limitations justified; inputs/outputs fully specified incl. format, units, domains, meaning, intended use.
- GitHub repository structure, commits and README: professional, clear, fully reproducible; logical folder hierarchy; consistent naming; README with purpose, usage, environment, dependencies; reproducible workflow; appropriate handling of data; evidence of branching and/or insightful commits.
- Inline comments, docstrings, HTML documentation: highly readable; inline comments clarify intent rather than restating code; docstrings follow a consistent style (NumPy) and fully describe parameters, return values, exceptions, usage; HTML documentation complete, well-structured, in sync with code.
- Validation and engineering credibility: rigorous validation; appropriate design; correct metrics; accurate results; comparison with ground truth/withheld data/benchmarks/physical constraints; identification of unsupported inputs and failure modes; honest limitations.
- Justification of computational approach: deep technical reasoning, credible baselines, evidence-based optimisation; quantitative analysis (accuracy, runtime, memory, robustness, scalability); evidence-supported improvements; thoughtful trade-offs; conclusions grounded in results.
- AI use and critical reflection: fully disclosed, critically evaluated, clearly subordinate to the student's own work; student clearly driving the AI process; verification of AI content; identification of errors; understanding; thoughtful reflection.
